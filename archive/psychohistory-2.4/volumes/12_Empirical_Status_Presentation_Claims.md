*What is known, what is not, and how the framework may be presented accurately*

Psychohistory 2.4 | Canonical storage revision | 6 October 2026

# 1. Current empirical status
As of 6 October 2026, aggregate Psychohistory predictive performance is not demonstrated. There is no legitimate overall Brier score, calibration slope, prospective false-alert rate or demonstrated incremental lead-time advantage. No G23 coupling has been promoted. October 2026 observations inspected during development are development-exposed.

# 2. Permitted presentation claims
- Psychohistory 2.4 is a proposed evidence-governed forecasting architecture.
- It integrates established statistical/stochastic/network methods under explicit point-in-time validation rules.
- Its mathematical components can be established even when their proposed combination is unvalidated.
- The framework is designed to return UNRESOLVED rather than force a prediction.
- Historical recurrence is treated as a testable predictor, not deterministic repetition.
- Current system-wide predictive advantage has not yet been demonstrated.

# 3. Claims not permitted
- It predicts history with proven accuracy.
- Its hazard follows from stochastic calculus.
- Hawkes excitation proves coordination or intent.
- Spectral changes prove systemic transition.
- Historical analogies establish destiny or fixed cycles.
- Multiple transforms or descendants are independent evidence.
- Current development observations are successful forecasts.

# 5. Simulation programme
Synthetic tests verify algorithms, not empirical truth. Required suites include null diffusion, OU recovery, jump processes, Hawkes subcritical/near-critical, graph fragmentation, common-driver pseudo-coupling, true coupling, recurrence with/without predictive value, vintage leakage traps and ancestry duplication traps.

# 6. Near-term falsification programme
Priorities are narrow outcome contracts with strong baselines: vintage-correct macro/construction, housing approvals/rates/prices, respiratory surveillance with epidemiological baselines, and exposure-network experiments. Sidecars are evaluated independently before integration.
