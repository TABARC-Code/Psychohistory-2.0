# Non-AI shock pilot: independent event anchors

Status: **research candidates only**. These anchors are not narrative time series,
registered forecasts, causal estimates, or evidence of predictive skill. Source
publication times and physical event times must remain separate.

## A. May 2024 geomagnetic storm: environmental and infrastructure shock

NOAA reports the first coronal mass ejection arrival on 10 May 2024 at
12:37 EDT (16:37 UTC), G3 conditions at 13:08 EDT (17:08 UTC), and G4
conditions at 13:39 EDT (17:39 UTC). An SWPC update was published at
22:38 UTC on 10 May, reporting G4 conditions. Earlier forecasts and watches
preceded the physical arrival, so a narrative leading the disturbance may
simply transmit a public scientific forecast.

Primary anchors:
- https://www.noaa.gov/stories/strong-geomagnetic-storm-reaches-earth-continues-through-weekend
- https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/news/geomagnetic-storming-likely-persist-weekend

Forward question: do official warnings and subsequent independent coverage
precede measurable protective actions, equipment incidents or communication
interruptions? Reverse question: do measured geomagnetic changes explain
later coverage once the earlier warning stream is controlled?

Controls: warning release time, Kp and observed storm level, local exposure,
longitude, time of day, infrastructure type, syndicated publishers and
coverage denominator. Distinguish physical onset from public warning onset.
A warning is not independent confirmation of a later storm outcome.

## B. July 2024 Windows security update outage: industrial and technical shock

The vendor's subsequent technical report states that a configuration update
was released on 19 July 2024 at **04:09 UTC** and remediated at **05:27 UTC**.
The technical report was published on 20 July; its later publication must not
be assigned to the 04:09 UTC information set.

Primary anchor:
- https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/falcon-update-for-windows-hosts-technical-details/

Forward question: how quickly did distinct publisher and community lineages
reproduce outage reports after independently timestamped service failures?
Reverse question: did independently sourced coverage and remediation guidance
precede recovery or secondary security behaviour, beyond the underlying
update and service-status information?

Controls: installed product exposure, service-provider dependence, outage
telemetry, coordinated newswire duplication, platform visibility, geography,
weekend timing and pre-existing IT incidents. Distinguish remediation advice,
rumours, opportunistic fraud and actual technical recovery. Shared source
material is not independent evidence of separate transmission.

## Measurement and evaluation contract before any modelling

1. Capture event time, first-available time, source, vintage, source ancestry,
   content ancestry, carrier, community and measurement quality separately.
   Missing lineage or exposure stays missing; never infer independence.
2. Define source-specific observation windows and publication-lag allowances
   *before* inspecting outcomes. Retain both warning-to-shock and
   shock-to-narrative directions, including zero and negative-control lags.
3. Measure exposure denominators and cross-community coverage. Compare
   existing G1 Hawkes/lineage diagnostics with inhomogeneous Poisson and
   shock-conditioned baselines; do not treat fitted excitation as causality.
4. Use rolling origins and embargo periods to prevent near-duplicate content
   and delayed revisions leaking across training and evaluation. Cluster
   shared publishers and ancestry when estimating uncertainty.
5. Test narrative-to-consequence models against contemporaneously available
   shock severity, calendar effects, conventional telemetry and a
   common-cause alternative. Preserve separate directional tests and adjust
   for the complete lag/domain hypothesis family.
6. Register any future forecast and outcome contract *before* its origin.
   No retrospective comparison, however interesting, admits G23 or proves
   prospective skill.

## Historical narrative data acquisition

The GDELT 2.0 event, mention and knowledge-graph archives provide
15-minute publication batches and are a candidate source of multilingual
coverage and source-network observations. Use the archived batch identifier
and first-seen time, not the date assigned to the described real-world event.
Current query results or subsequently revised classifications are not proof
of what the system could have known at a 2024 forecast origin. Preserve
original file vintages, batch checksums, collection gaps, language coverage
and source duplication before attempting Hawkes exposure estimates.

Source documentation: https://gdeltproject.org/data.html
