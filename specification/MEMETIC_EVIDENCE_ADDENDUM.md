# Psychohistory 2.4 — Memetic Evidence and Social Resonance Amendment

This amendment refines the Evidence Independence and G1 contracts. It is normative for the executable implementation from this revision onward and must be incorporated into the next canonical specification revision.

## Claim-relative evidence independence

Evidence independence is not a universal property of an observation. It is relative to the claim being tested.

If multiple observations descend from the same content lineage, they are ancestry-linked for claims about content truth, origin and attribution. They must not be counted as independent confirmations of those claims merely because they occur on different accounts, platforms or publications.

The same observations remain distinct propagation events when the claim concerns diffusion, social penetration, synchronisation, mutation, persistence, topology, cross-community spread or coordination. Deduplicating them from G1 solely because their content is copied destroys information about social behaviour.

Thus, in general:

    I_content != I_propagation

and shared content ancestry can coexist with multiple genuine propagation observations.

## Meme and carrier separation

A meme is not identified with its carrier. The same narrative object may propagate through speech, print, broadcast media, news sites, forums or social platforms. Carrier transitions are themselves observations.

    Meme x Carrier

Cross-carrier movement should be represented by a transition kernel K(c_i -> c_j, delta_t) where the data permit it.

## G1 decomposition

G1 is extended to preserve distinct inference tasks:

    G1 = (dynamics, source_tree, genealogy_mutation,
          downstream_influence, coordination, social_resonance)

A successful process fit does not automatically admit any component as predictive evidence.

## Social resonance

Memetic behaviour is both communication and behavioural telemetry. Adoption, rejection, parody, mutation, persistence, independent recreation, endogenous reproduction and cross-community spread may contain information about explicit attitudes and latent social states.

Repetition is not mechanically equivalent to endorsement. A meme may be reproduced because it is believed, rejected, mocked, feared, inverted, socially rewarded or otherwise salient.

Latent social resonance is therefore an inferred variable, not a direct measurement of a collective subconscious. Any interpretation of latent attitudes requires uncertainty, competing explanations and validation against independent behavioural evidence.

## Coordination and attribution

Propagation, coordination and attribution are separate inference problems.

Coordination requires an explicit null model using appropriate evidence such as temporal synchrony, semantic similarity, actor/network topology, source concentration, cross-carrier timing and amplification structure. Volume alone is insufficient.

Attribution must not be inferred merely because coordination is detected. At minimum, the system should preserve competing hypotheses for:

1. organic endogenous propagation;
2. domestic coordinated amplification;
3. foreign coordinated amplification;
4. a common broadcaster or common external event;
5. algorithmic amplification;
6. sampling or measurement artefact.

A valid output may therefore support coordinated amplification while leaving foreign-state attribution unresolved.

## Admission

Propagation and resonance diagnostics are candidates until they demonstrate incremental predictive value under the normal point-in-time, multiplicity, ancestry, uncertainty and held-out validation contracts. Memetic observations are not granted predictive status merely because they are numerous.
